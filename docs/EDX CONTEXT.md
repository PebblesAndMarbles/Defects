My plasma dry etch module opens hard mask on back end wafer processing flows. We Process a series of over 10 Layers each of which comes right after a lithography patterning operation. Immediately following our operation is a wet etch step that presumably removes and somewhat cleans up wafers that we etch. Soon after the wet etch operation The wafers get scanned for inline defects. The AME (my module) process operations MT5 and MT6 get Inline scanned at defects Measurement layers called 8M5CL and 8M6CL, respectively.  See \\orshfs.intel.com\ORAnalysis$\1276_MAODATA\Config\etch\AME\tbatson\Defects\BE\INLINE_PIPELINE_DESIGN.md for description of pipeline for inline prod wafer defect metrics, coordinates, and images.  In addition to production wafers, Test wafers regularly get processed on our chambers and then get scanned immediately without any wet etch operation using so called SURF scan station monitor.   See SURF_SCAN_PIPELINE_DESIGN.md for surf scan test wafer defect metrics, coordinates and images.  In addition to these items, test wafer results of interest can also be manually sent for edx elemental analysis where the SURF scan particles are analyzed for elemental composition.  Yttria is one key element of interest because it is a major component of the etch chamber part coating, and presence of yttria is believed to suggest a deteriorating kit.  As part of the surf scan pipeline, EDX data are handled by stacking occurence of all elements into a csv that groups Yttria with Phosphorous and Osmium, since the peaks are virtually identical in location as Yttria peak and are thought to be indicative of  part degradation.
