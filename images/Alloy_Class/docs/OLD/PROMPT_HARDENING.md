1. evidence_check_inset_surface_lines
Look at the SiO substrate immediately adjacent to the defect boundary. Is the SiO surface visibly extended, bulged, or inset in a direction consistent with the defect blocking an etch? These lines appear as thin ridges or topology changes on the SiO surface layer itself — smooth, planar distortions of the substrate.
Critical discrimination: the surface lines must be on the SiO substrate, not on the particle itself. A particle surface has granular, irregular, or non-planar texture. If what appears to be a surface line is the particle edge or particle surface rather than the surrounding SiO substrate, it does not qualify. Score yes if a smooth SiO-surface topology change is present adjacent to the defect boundary. Subtle but real detections count as yes — do not downgrade a detected feature to unclear just because it is small.

****	I am not sure what 'smooth, planar distortions of the substrate' is specifying.  basically, the inset surface line is always meant to represent appearance of the substrate in region that it should not be.  Crucially the model needs to be able to distinguish whether the substrate is extending into a comparator below a blocking defect or if the defect has landed post etch, in which case there certainly should not be SiO inside a comparator feature.  Depending on the particle size, the extension of an SiO surface into a comparator can either be obvious, ranging from a thickness (measuring distance from dark etched comparator region to a material defect boundary) of a few pixels to even tens or a hundred.  Most often, misclassified SMPs will have an inset surface line that is quite small, and its inference may need to be made using either bright or darkfield image, depending on orientation of the particle and location with respect to light source.  An inset surface line can also be inferred somewhat more confidently when the blocking material defect itself shows inset boundary lines corresponding to different layers of stack material.



2. evidence_check_boundary_conformance
Examine each edge of the defect where it meets or abuts a comparator or trench boundary. Is any edge of the defect sharply cut at the expected location of a structure boundary in a way that cannot be explained by the particle simply resting next to the structure? Key signatures: one edge of the defect is cleanly terminated at a trench or comparator wall; or the defect extends slightly into a structure with a contrast line in the material matching the expected etch boundary. A single qualifying edge is sufficient — score yes even if only one edge shows this pattern. Approximate or gradual edges do not qualify. Score yes if the geometry is precise and unexplained by coincidence.

*** I have a hunch that pre etch Defects exhibiting boundary conformance can also still jut ever so slightly into a comparator, Without showing sunken residual material. I would estimate that a rule of thumb could be that a small portion of the conformed particle on the order of maybe 15% in the direction normal from the boundary line and into the comparator with respect to the total length that conforms to the boundary can present itself and still be considered a pre-etch (BEEP) particle.

3. evidence_check_sunken_residual
Is material visible recessed inside a comparator or trench at a depth below the surface level, with tone or contrast that differs from the surface defect? This presents as contrast lightening or a tonal shift inside a comparator, often with a boundary line where surface material transitions to recessed material. Score yes if present at any scale. Score unclear if a tonal shift is suggested but cannot be confirmed.
