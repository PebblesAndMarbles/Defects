[CONFIRMED] Calibration guidance to add to the prompt text (not a vocabulary change --
same 4 options, tighter instruction for when to use each):
  - Reserve "Circle" for a close-to-true circular/disc outline only. Model confidence on
    "circle" calls was measurably lower than other coarse_shape calls in v2 (e.g. 0.78 vs.
    a typical 0.86-0.96), suggesting it defaults to "circle" under uncertainty rather than
    a more honest "round" or "angular" -- explicitly tell it to prefer "round" or "angular"
    when not clearly circular.
  - Visual anchor for "Circle," from your own reference examples outside the test set:
    the outline must be smooth and symmetric all the way around, with no flat/faceted
    segment or notch anywhere on the boundary. Your accepted boundary case
    (AME425_PM5, D6231600 W023 ID1) has one distinctly flatter facet on its lower-left
    side and is the maximum deviation still acceptable as "circle" -- anything with more
    asymmetry than that (more than one facet, a clear notch, an elongated axis) should
    fall to "round" or "angular" instead. Your four "rigorous circle" examples
    (AME423_PM4 D6182110 W448 ID303; AME425_PM4 D6182330 W050 ID19; AME425_PM4 D6182330
    W050 ID11; AME411_PM1 D6192710 W018 ID182525) are all small, compact, and uniformly
    smooth-edged with zero visible facets -- the clean end of the spectrum.
    Cross-check against the actual pilot set: SMP_PILOT_013 (model's own description
    noted "a slight central depression") and SMP_PILOT_027 (notched, lowest-confidence
    circle call at 0.78) both show more irregularity than even the accepted boundary
    case above, supporting that both were likely over-called and should have been
    "round."
  - "Round" = soft/blobby outline, no circle-level regularity and no straight/angular
    edges.
  - "Angular" = at least one relatively straight edge or corner-like feature is visible.
  - "Clumped" = reserve for ONE outline that itself looks fused/multi-lobed (multiple
    bumps/lobes on a single connected silhouette, ambiguous whether it's one particle or
    several merged). Do NOT use "Clumped" just because defect_count > 1 with clearly
    separate, individually-outlined particles -- describe each particle's own shape in
    that case (defect_count already captures the multiplicity). [Finding: SMP_PILOT_030
    had 2 clearly separate particles, correctly counted, but coarse_shape was still
    "clumped" -- should have been the primary particle's actual outline shape, e.g.
    angular.]

Definition tightening (adapted from BEEP_Evidence copy 2.txt's "Comparator occlusion" /
"inset surface lines" sections -- observational vocabulary only):
  - on_sio: defect either (1) does not touch a comparator's real or inferred boundary at all, or (2) touches that boundary but does not cross over/into it (stays fully on the SiO side).  
  - in_comparator: defect is fully within a comparator's real or inferred boundary.
  - spanning_both: defect's own edge crosses at least one comparator's real or inferred
    boundary line (part of the defect on the SiO side, part on the comparator side).
  [Findings this would target: SMP_PILOT_008 -- particle visibly straddles a trench line,
  touching SiO on both sides, but was labeled on_sio. SMP_PILOT_005 -- both particles sit
  inside via footprints per the model's own free-text description, but the field said
  spanning_both, disagreeing with its own description.]
